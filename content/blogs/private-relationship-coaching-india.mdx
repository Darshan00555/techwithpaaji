## Why Privacy Matters So Much in India

In India, relationships are not just private matters. They exist within family systems, social networks, and community contexts that have strong opinions about how relationships should look and function.

Admitting that your marriage is struggling means risking: family interference (well-meaning or otherwise), social judgment, changes in how others treat you, and sometimes even pressure toward decisions you're not ready to make.

This is not irrational. These social dynamics are real.

The result: millions of Indians suffer through relationship problems alone, without support, because the cost of seeking help feels too high.

## What to Ask Before You Book Anything

Privacy claims are easy to make. These questions establish whether they mean anything, and they apply to any provider rather than only to us.

**What is recorded, and where is it stored?** Session notes, if kept, should be encrypted and accessible only to the coach. Ask whether sessions themselves are recorded — the answer should be no by default, and any exception should require your explicit consent each time.

**Who else can see it?** In a solo practice, nobody. In a larger platform, potentially administrators and support staff. Neither is wrong, but you should know which arrangement you are in before you disclose anything.

**How long is it kept, and can it be deleted?** A clear retention period and a deletion process on request are reasonable to expect. A provider who has not considered the question is telling you something.

**What appears on the payment record?** This matters more than people expect in shared-account households. Ask what the transaction descriptor says, since a line item naming a counselling service on a joint statement is the most common way privacy is lost.

**What happens if a family member contacts you?** The answer should be that no information is confirmed or denied, including whether you are a client at all.

### The Limits of Confidentiality

No one should promise absolute confidentiality, and a provider who does is either careless or misinformed.

The standard exceptions are risk of serious harm to yourself or another person, and legal compulsion such as a court order. These are narrow and rarely invoked, but they exist, and a credible practitioner will state them plainly at the outset rather than after something has been said.
